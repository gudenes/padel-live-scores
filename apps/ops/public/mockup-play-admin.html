Market Templates

Templates are generators, not markets. Each one watches the calendar and instantiates markets automatically — a season of Premier events becomes a few hundred markets without anyone authoring them by hand.

Operators never write queries. Every template points at a resolver — a named, unit-tested function in the code registry — and fills in its parameters. Free-text SQL in an admin panel is how a market silently settles wrong and pays out thousands of Guacas.

Why the cap is this low. The binding constraint is liquidity per market, not database size. At ~40 daily actives making ~120 trades, 300 markets gives 0.4 trades each and every price sits frozen at its seed. Twelve markets gives ~10 trades each and the price actually moves. Rule of thumb: open markets ≈ daily actives ÷ 10 — raise it as users arrive, not as matches do.

    Economy

    Guacas are play money, but the supply still has to be governed. If balances inflate faster than the user base, a 250 G stake stops meaning anything and every decision in the app gets cheaper.

    Inflation is running hot. Net issuance is +8.5%/week against circulation — at this rate the money supply doubles in about 8 weeks, while the user base does not. The typical stake has already fallen from 4.1% to 2.2% of the median balance. Levers, in order of bluntness: cut the daily stipend, add a real sink, or shorten the season.

    AMM subsidy sizing

    LMSR

    The good news: an LMSR market maker's loss is mathematically bounded, so the worst case is knowable before a single trade happens. You pick the ceiling; b follows.

    max loss = b · ln(2) = b × 0.6931  (binary market)
    b = 5,000 / 0.6931 = 7,213
    worst case per market = 5,000 G
    daily ceiling = 20 markets × 5,000 = 100,000 G
    Max loss per market (G)
    Derived b

    Higher b = deeper liquidity, prices move less per trade, costs more. Lower b = a thin market a single 2,000 G order can swing 20 points. At our volume, err low — a price that moves is the entire product.
